How to Prepare for GATE while in B.Tech

📅 Published: 28 June 2026 ✍️ Written by: Er. Prince Kumar (BEU Mentor & ACB Team) ⏱️ 5 min read

Why the GATE Examination is a Game-Changer for BEU Students

The Graduate Aptitude Test in Engineering (GATE) is one of the most prestigious and empowering examinations for engineering undergraduates in India. Jointly administered by the Indian Institute of Science (IISc) and the seven Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs), a top GATE score is the gateway to:

  • Direct Public Sector Recruitment (PSUs): High-paying government executive jobs in Maharatna and Navratna enterprises like IOCL, ONGC, NTPC, PowerGrid, BHEL, HPCL, and GAIL with initial CTCs ranging from ₹14 LPA to ₹22 LPA.
  • Postgraduate Admissions: Master of Technology (M.Tech) and direct Ph.D. admissions at premier institutions (IISc Bangalore, IITs, NITs) with a monthly MHRD fellowship of ₹12,400.
  • Research & Development Careers: Opportunities at premier national scientific organizations like BARC, ISRO, and DRDO.

For students in Bihar government engineering colleges, where on-campus core placement opportunities may be limited, GATE provides a level, meritocratic playing field to compete nationally.

---

Understanding the GATE Exam Pattern & Marking Scheme

The GATE examination is a 3-hour computer-based test comprising 65 questions totaling 100 marks:

  • General Aptitude (GA): 10 Questions = 15 Marks (Verbal Ability, Numerical Ability, Spatial Aptitude).
  • Engineering Mathematics: 10–12 Questions = 13 to 15 Marks.
  • Core Engineering Discipline: 43–45 Questions = 70 to 72 Marks.

Types of Questions:

  1. Multiple Choice Questions (MCQ): 1 or 2 marks with negative marking (1/3rd mark deducted for 1-mark questions; 2/3rd mark deducted for 2-mark questions).
  2. Multiple Select Questions (MSQ): One or more options can be correct. No negative marking, but no partial credit.
  3. Numerical Answer Type (NAT): Numerical value entered via virtual numeric keypad. No negative marking.

4-Semester Strategic Roadmap for BEU Students

Starting in your 3rd year (5th semester) is the optimal timeline to build deep conceptual clarity without burning out:

Semester 4 (Pre-Preparation Phase): Mathematics & Core Basics

  • Complete the Engineering Mathematics syllabus. Linear Algebra, Calculus, and Differential Equations carry 13-15 marks across all disciplines.
  • Focus on the basic core subjects of your branch taught during 2nd year (e.g., Strength of Materials in Civil, Data Structures in CSE, Thermodynamics in Mechanical, Network Theory in Electrical).

Semester 5 (Core Subject Deep-Dive):

  • Dedicate 3 to 4 hours daily outside college hours.
  • Tackle the highest-weightage core subjects of your branch. Study theory from standard textbooks or free government NPTEL video lectures by IIT professors.
  • Immediately after finishing a topic, solve the last 20 years of GATE Previous Year Questions for that specific topic.

Semester 6 (Completion & Test Series Launch):

  • Complete the remaining secondary subjects of the syllabus by October.
  • Enroll in a reputable nationwide GATE online test series. Begin by taking Topic-wise Tests and Subject-wise Tests.
  • Build your personal GATE Short Notes Binder (condense every subject into 15 to 20 pages of formulas, boundary conditions, and standard trick concepts).

Semester 7 (Revision, Full-Length Mocks & Error Analysis):

  • Between November and January, transition entirely to full-length 3-hour mock exams.
  • Write at least 25 to 35 full mock tests during the exact exam time slot (9:30 AM to 12:30 PM or 2:30 PM to 5:30 PM) to align your biological clock.
  • Maintain an Error Logbook: Record every formula forgotten, numerical miscalculation, and conceptual misunderstanding. Review this logbook before every subsequent mock test.

Balancing University Semester Exams with GATE

Many students make the fatal mistake of abandoning their college coursework for GATE, resulting in backlogs or a low CGPA that disqualifies them from PSU recruitment (which typically mandates 65% or 6.5+ CGPA).

  • Leverage Syllabus Overlap: More than 70% of BEU 3rd and 4th year semester syllabuses align directly with GATE topics. When preparing for university mid-sems and end-sems, study the topics using standard GATE reference books and solve numerical problems.
  • Use University Exams for Deep Derivations: While GATE tests quick numerical results, BEU exams test step-by-step derivations. Knowing the derivation of a formula ensures you never forget its underlying assumptions during GATE NAT questions.
  • Allocate Dedicated Morning Hours: Study for GATE early in the morning (6:00 AM to 9:00 AM) before attending college lectures. This ensures your core GATE study is completed before campus distractions arise.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q1: Are 3rd-year engineering students eligible to write the GATE examination?

Yes. Under current GATE regulations, students currently enrolled in the 3rd year or higher of any undergraduate degree program are fully eligible to appear for the GATE examination.

Q2: For how many years is a GATE scorecard valid?

A GATE scorecard is valid for 3 years from the date of announcement of results for admission to postgraduate academic programs (M.Tech/MS/Ph.D.). However, for PSU recruitment drives, most PSUs consider only the GATE score of the current recruitment year.

Q3: Can I crack GATE without joining expensive coaching institutes?

Absolutely. Thousands of students achieve top 500 ranks through disciplined self-study utilizing free NPTEL video lectures, standard engineering textbooks, previous 30 years of GATE question papers, and a dedicated online test series.
