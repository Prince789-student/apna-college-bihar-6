BEU Passing Marks Criteria and Grading System Explained

📅 Published: 28 June 2026 ✍️ Written by: Er. Prince Kumar (BEU Mentor & ACB Team) ⏱️ 5 min read

Demystifying the BEU Evaluation & Grading System

For engineering students enrolled under the Bihar Engineering University (BEU), understanding the official examination passing rules, grading scales, and credit architectures is essential. Misconceptions about passing marks can lead to avoidable backlogs, academic probation, or unexpected delays in degree completion.

Whether you are a 1st-year fresher trying to navigate your first end-semester exams or a senior calculating your cumulative CGPA for campus placements, this guide provides the definitive breakdown of BEU evaluation standards.

---

The Dual Passing Threshold: The Golden Rule

In BEU, every theory course is evaluated through two distinct components:

  1. Continuous Internal Assessment (Sessional): 30 Marks (Conducted by your college department through mid-semester exams, class tests, attendance, and assignments).
  2. End-Semester External Examination: 70 Marks (Conducted centrally by BEU across official university examination centers).
  • Total per Subject: 100 Marks.

To successfully pass any theory subject, you must satisfy both of the following conditions independently:

Component Maximum Marks Minimum Passing Percentage Minimum Marks Required
External End-Semester Exam 70 Marks 35% 24.5 Marks (Strictly evaluated as 25)
Combined Aggregate (Internal + External) 100 Marks 40% 40 Marks

Understanding the Real-World Implications:

  • Scenario A (Pass): Student scores 26/70 in Externals and 18/30 in Internals. Total = 44/100. The student passed the 35% external mark and the 40% overall mark. Result: PASS.
  • Scenario B (Fail due to External Rule): Student scores 20/70 in Externals and 28/30 in Internals. Total = 48/100. Even though the overall score is 48% (well above 40%), the student failed the 35% external threshold (scored below 25). Result: FAIL / BACKLOG (F Grade).
  • Scenario C (Fail due to Aggregate Rule): Student scores 25/70 in Externals and 12/30 in Internals. Total = 37/100. The student cleared the external threshold but failed the 40% aggregate requirement. Result: FAIL / BACKLOG (F Grade).
Laboratory / Practical Passing Rule: For practical laboratory examinations and viva-voce courses (usually 50 or 100 marks), students must secure a minimum of 50% marks to pass.

---

The Official 10-Point Letter Grading System

BEU follows the standard UGC-recommended 10-point letter grading framework. Your performance in each subject is converted into a Letter Grade and an equivalent Grade Point:

Letter Grade Performance Category Grade Point (GP) Percentage Marks Range
O Outstanding 10 90% and above
E Excellent 9 80% to 89%
A Very Good 8 70% to 79%
B Good 7 60% to 69%
C Fair 6 50% to 59%
D Pass (Average) 5 40% to 49%
F Fail (Backlog) 0 Below 40% (or <35% in External)
I Incomplete 0 Course requirements not completed

---

How SGPA and CGPA are Calculated

1. Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA)

Your SGPA measures academic performance in a single semester. It is the weighted average of the grade points earned across all registered subjects:

$\text{SGPA} = \frac{\sum (C_i \times G_i)}{\sum C_i}$

  • Where $C_i$ = Number of credits assigned to subject $i$.
  • Where $G_i$ = Grade points secured in subject $i$.

Example Calculation:

Suppose you take 5 subjects in a semester with the following results:

  • Mathematics (4 Credits): Grade 'A' (8 GP) → $4 \times 8 = 32$
  • Physics (4 Credits): Grade 'E' (9 GP) → $4 \times 9 = 36$
  • Basic Electrical (3 Credits): Grade 'B' (7 GP) → $3 \times 7 = 21$
  • Physics Lab (1.5 Credits): Grade 'O' (10 GP) → $1.5 \times 10 = 15$
  • Electrical Lab (1.5 Credits): Grade 'A' (8 GP) → $1.5 \times 8 = 12$
  • Total Credit Points: $32 + 36 + 21 + 15 + 12 = 116$
  • Total Credits: $4 + 4 + 3 + 1.5 + 1.5 = 14$
  • SGPA: $116 / 14 = 8.28$

2. Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA)

Your CGPA measures cumulative academic performance from Semester 1 up to the current semester:

$\text{CGPA} = \frac{\sum (\text{Total Credit Points earned in each semester})}{\sum (\text{Total Credits across all semesters})}$

---

BEU Official Formula for Converting CGPA to Percentage

When filling out forms for competitive exams (UPSC, BPSC, GATE) or private sector jobs that require percentage representation, BEU applies the following standardized formula:

$\text{Equivalent Percentage of Marks} = \text{CGPA} \times 10$

(Note: Certain older university transcripts referenced $(\text{CGPA} - 0.75) \times 10$. Always verify the conversion note printed on the reverse side of your official BEU grade card).

---

Carry-Over (Backlog) and Promotion Regulations

  • Odd/Even Semester System: If you receive an 'F' grade in an odd semester course (Sem 1, 3, 5, 7), you may re-appear for the external examination during the subsequent odd semester carry-over examination. Even semester backlogs are similarly cleared during even semester cycles.
  • Year-Back (Academic Promotion) Criteria: To be promoted from the 1st year to the 2nd year, or from the 2nd year to the 3rd year, students must earn a minimum threshold of total accumulated credits (typically at least 50% of the total credits assigned for that academic year). Accumulating too many active backlogs will trigger a year-back.

---

Scrutiny & Re-Evaluation Procedures

If you believe your answer sheet was evaluated incorrectly:

  1. Apply for Scrutiny / Re-checking through the university examination portal within 15 days of official result publication.
  2. Pay the prescribed scrutiny fee per subject online.
  3. Scrutiny involves re-totalling of marks, checking for unassessed answers, and verification against evaluation keys.

---

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q1: If I score 24 marks in externals and 28 in internals, can a teacher give me 1 grace mark?

Grace marks are governed strictly by university examination board statutes during moderation. Evaluators cannot arbitrarily add grace marks on individual sheets. If university moderation rules apply, minor shortfalls may be adjusted; otherwise, 24.5 marks is the non-negotiable floor.

Q2: Do backlog marks appear on the final degree certificate?

Your final 4-year degree certificate lists your overall division and cumulative CGPA. Detailed semester-wise grade sheets reflect the semester in which each course was successfully cleared.
