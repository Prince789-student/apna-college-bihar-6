BEU B.Tech 1st Year Syllabus Complete Guide

📅 Published: 28 June 2026 ✍️ Written by: Er. Prince Kumar (BEU Mentor & ACB Team) ⏱️ 5 min read

Welcome to Bihar Engineering University: The 1st Year Foundation

The first year of your Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech) journey at Bihar Engineering University (BEU) is the academic bedrock of your engineering degree. Unlike subsequent semesters where you dive into departmental specializations (Civil, Mechanical, Electrical, CSE), the 1st-year curriculum is standardized across almost all disciplines under AICTE model curriculum guidelines.

Scoring a high Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) in your first two semesters provides a strong academic cushion that safeguards your overall CGPA throughout your 4-year degree.

This comprehensive guide details the subject distribution, unit-by-unit syllabus breakdown, recommended reference books, and practical strategies to master the 1st-year curriculum.

---

The Group System Explained: Group A vs. Group B

To optimize laboratory access and faculty allocation across 38 government engineering colleges, BEU divides branches into two operational groups. In Semester 1, Group A takes one set of subjects while Group B takes the other; in Semester 2, the groups swap subjects:

  • Group A (Physics Group): Typically encompasses Mechanical Engineering, Civil Engineering, and allied disciplines.
  • Group B (Chemistry Group): Typically encompasses Computer Science & Engineering (CSE), Information Technology (IT), Electrical Engineering (EE), and Electronics & Communication (ECE).

---

Detailed Subject & Unit Breakdown

1. Engineering Mathematics - I & II (Calculus & Linear Algebra)

Mathematics forms the backbone of all engineering problem solving:

  • Unit 1: Differential Calculus: Rolle's Theorem, Mean Value Theorems, Taylor’s and Maclaurin’s theorems, indeterminate forms, and L'Hôpital's rule.
  • Unit 2: Multivariable Calculus (Differentiation): Partial derivatives, total derivatives, Euler’s theorem for homogeneous functions, maxima and minima of functions of two variables, Lagrange multipliers.
  • Unit 3: Matrices & Linear Algebra: Rank of a matrix by echelon forms, system of linear equations, eigenvalues, eigenvectors, Cayley-Hamilton Theorem, diagonalization of matrices.
  • Unit 4: Multivariable Calculus (Integration): Double and triple integrals, change of order of integration, change of variables to polar and cylindrical coordinates, applications to area and volume.
  • Unit 5: Vector Calculus: Gradient, divergence, curl, line, surface, and volume integrals, Green’s, Stokes’, and Gauss Divergence theorems.
  • Recommended Book: Higher Engineering Mathematics by B.S. Grewal or Advanced Engineering Mathematics by Erwin Kreyszig.

2. Engineering Physics

Focuses on physical principles essential for modern hardware and materials science:

  • Unit 1: Wave Optics: Interference (Newton’s rings, thin films), Diffraction (Fraunhofer diffraction at single and double slits, diffraction grating), Polarization (Brewster’s law, double refraction).
  • Unit 2: Quantum Mechanics: Introduction to quantum mechanics, wave-particle duality, de Broglie hypothesis, Heisenberg uncertainty principle, Schrödinger’s time-dependent and time-independent wave equations, particle in a 1D box.
  • Unit 3: Lasers & Fiber Optics: Spontaneous and stimulated emission, population inversion, Ruby and He-Ne lasers, optical fiber principles, acceptance angle, numerical aperture, attenuation in fibers.
  • Unit 4: Electromagnetism: Maxwell's equations in differential and integral forms, electromagnetic wave equation in free space, Poynting vector.
  • Recommended Book: Engineering Physics by H.K. Malik and A.K. Singh.

3. Engineering Chemistry

Emphasizes industrial engineering chemistry, corrosion control, and materials science:

  • Unit 1: Atomic & Molecular Structure: Molecular orbital theory (MOT) for diatomic molecules, band theory of solids.
  • Unit 2: Spectroscopic Techniques: Principles of UV-Visible, Infrared (IR), and NMR spectroscopy.
  • Unit 3: Water Technology: Hardness of water, boiler troubles (scales, sludges, priming, foaming), water softening methods (Zeolite and Ion-Exchange processes).
  • Unit 4: Polymers & Nanomaterials: Classification, polymerization mechanisms, synthesis of industrially important polymers (Nylon, Bakelite), properties and engineering applications of nanomaterials.
  • Recommended Book: Engineering Chemistry by P.C. Jain and Monika Jain.

4. Basic Electrical Engineering (BEE)

A conceptual subject covering fundamental electrical circuitry:

  • Unit 1: DC Circuits: Kirchhoff's Laws (KCL/KVL), Mesh and Nodal analysis, Superposition theorem, Thevenin’s and Norton’s theorems, Maximum Power Transfer theorem.
  • Unit 2: AC Circuits: Representation of sinusoidal waveforms, peak and RMS values, phasor representation, series and parallel R-L-C circuits, resonance, power factor.
  • Unit 3: Transformers: Ideal and practical single-phase transformers, equivalent circuit, losses in transformers, regulation and efficiency calculation.
  • Unit 4: Electrical Machines: Construction and operating principles of DC machines, single-phase and three-phase induction motors.
  • Recommended Book: Basic Electrical Engineering by D.P. Kothari and I.J. Nagrath.

5. Programming for Problem Solving (PPS - C Language)

The foundation of software literacy for all engineers:

  • Unit 1: Introduction to Computing & C Syntax: Data types, operators, operator precedence, expressions.
  • Unit 2: Control Structures: Conditional branching (if-else, switch-case), iterative loops (for, while, do-while).
  • Unit 3: Arrays & Strings: 1D and 2D arrays, matrix manipulation, string handling functions.
  • Unit 4: Functions & Recursion: Call by value, call by reference, recursive algorithms.
  • Unit 5: Pointers & Memory Management: Pointer arithmetic, arrays of pointers, dynamic memory allocation (malloc, calloc, free), structures, unions, and file I/O operations.
  • Recommended Book: Programming in ANSI C by E. Balagurusamy.

6. Engineering Graphics & Design

Teaches spatial reasoning and engineering technical drafting:

  • Principles of Engineering Graphics, Scales (Plain, Diagonal).
  • Orthographic Projections of Points, Lines, and Planes.
  • Projections of Regular Solids (Prisms, Pyramids, Cylinders, Cones).
  • Isometric Views and Projections.
  • Introduction to Computer Aided Drafting (AutoCAD software interface).
  • Recommended Book: Engineering Drawing by N.D. Bhatt.

5 Actionable Tips to Secure a 9.0+ SGPA in First Year

  1. Prioritize Mathematics Daily: Engineering Mathematics carries 4 credits. Solving 5 problems every evening prevents mid-term panic.
  2. Master the C Programming Lab: Do not merely memorize code. Type and debug C programs yourself in the lab. Understanding pointers and arrays early gives you an immense head start.
  3. Punctual Practical Lab Records: Practical lab sessional exams carry 50 marks per subject. Maintain neat observation notebooks and viva notes to lock in top sessional grades.
  4. Download BEU 1st Year PYQs Early: Review past papers from the ACB PYQ Section after finishing each unit to understand question formats.
  5. English & Communication Skills Matter: Do not underestimate the Communication Skills course. Earning an 'O' grade here is straightforward and boosts your aggregate SGPA significantly.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q1: Is the 1st year syllabus identical across all 38 colleges under BEU?

Yes. Bihar Engineering University prescribes a single, unified curriculum and conducts centralized end-semester examinations across all affiliated government and private engineering institutes.

Q2: What happens if I get a backlog in a 1st-year subject?

You will be promoted to the 2nd year provided you clear the minimum credit threshold mandated by BEU regulations. Backlog papers can be cleared during the carry-over exams in the odd/even semester cycle.

Q3: Where can I download official BEU 1st-year notes?

You can access handwritten, unit-wise notes compiled by top-ranking students and professors in the Apna College Bihar Notes Section.
